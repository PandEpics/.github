## Pandemic Epistemics ("PandEpics")

During the COVID-19 pandemic, many statistical models were developed to estimate key measures such as transmission rates and to forecast possible future scenarios based on the available case count and wastewater data. These results informed public health planning and policy. However, these studies could only use the data that were available at the time, and very few (if any) of them were revisited as more information became available. Additionally, these studies were published as soon as the authors were able; earlier results may have been beneficial for public health decision making.

This project will re-run published COVID-19 models using data from different points in the pandemic, both earlier and later, using novel extensions of meta-analytic techniques that incorporate time. This will allow us to test whether their estimates became better with additional data, or would have still been useful with less data (as though the study were performed earlier). The results will help determine which modelling strategies are most effective in real-time outbreak situations, improving our ability to produce timely and accurate information during future public health emergencies.

A project called The COVID-19 Data Hub keeps snapshots of data collected through the pandemic. These snapshots are *not* updated if data errors were found later; they represent an accurate snapshot of the knowledge we had at the time. Any paper that uses these data and makes their could available would make it very easy to replicate their results at a different point in the pandemic.

### Basic Studies

- Literature review for analyses based on publicly available data that contributed to public health decisions, with preference for studies that shared their analysis code.
- Replicate analyses that use COVID-19 Data Hub and share their code.
	- Use different snapshots to investigate how their results would have changed.
	- There are *many, many* studies, so many students could work on this at once!

### Advanced Research (Master's and Above)

- Draw from meta-analysis methods to properly account for the variance, and develop new methodology to quantify the change in uncertainty over time.
- If we replicate a study on, say, January 1st 2022 and again on February 1st 2022, the two models share most of the same data and their results *should* be similar. Accounting for this "filtration" is a major part of the methodological development.
